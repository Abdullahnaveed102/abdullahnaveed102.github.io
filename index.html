About me

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Engineering at the University of Florida, advised by Dr. Guanpeng Li in the Dependable Systems Lab. My research lies at the intersection of dependable machine learning, high-performance computing, and scientific data systems.

I study how large language models and HPC applications behave under hardware and software faults, and I design low-overhead mechanisms that make these systems more reliable. At Argonne National Laboratory, I am advised by Dr. Sheng Di on hardware–software co-design for scientific data compression.

My current interests include:

  • Dependable AI: fault characterization and low-overhead protection for LLM inference, fine-tuning, and training
  • Scientific data systems: hardware-accelerated compression for large-scale scientific datasets
  • HPC resilience: compiler and runtime techniques for detecting silent data corruption

Ongoing research

Dependable large language models

Characterizing soft-error behavior across LLM inference, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and pretraining, while developing lightweight runtime detection.

Scientific data compression

Co-designing hardware and software compression methods for data-intensive scientific workflows.

Compiler-based HPC resilience

Using LLVM-based fault injection and selective runtime monitoring to detect silent data corruption in scientific applications.
